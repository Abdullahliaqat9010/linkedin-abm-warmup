# The 21-day LinkedIn ABM warm-up

The idea: by the time you ask for anything, you're a familiar name who has
already been useful. Every touch should be worth something on its own, even if
they never buy.

## Schedule

| When | Step | User does | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Pick | Approve 20–30 accounts with fit + a live signal | The signal is the reason to show up. Fit alone is a list. |
| Day 0 | Map | Approve 3–5 people per account | One contact stalls. A committee buys. |
| Days 1–7 | Get seen | Follow, view profiles, 2–3 real comments per person | By the time you connect, your name feels familiar. |
| Days 8–14 | Connect | Send requests: blank, or one line on their content | A pitch in the note gets ignored. |
| Days 15–21 | Give first | Share one thing specific to their company | Earns the right to ask. |
| Day 21 | Ask | Short DM tied to the signal, one question | Reads as a follow-up, not a cold message. |
| Ongoing | Multi-thread | Champion engages → warm the decision maker the same way | Deals need more than one voice inside. |

## Why 21 days

Signals go stale. Hiring plans change, budgets get allocated, new leaders
settle in. Three weeks is long enough to build familiarity and short enough
for the original signal to still be relevant when you ask.

## Picking accounts

- 20–30 per cycle. More than that and the comments stop being specific.
- Fit: matches ICP on industry, size, region and buyer.
- Signal (examples — adapt to the offer):
  - Hiring for a role your product supports or replaces
  - Funding round in the last 60 days
  - New leader in the buyer's seat (first 90 days = new priorities)
  - Launching into a new market or product line
  - Public complaint or question about the problem you solve
  - Using or leaving a competitor / adjacent tool
- Prefer accounts with two converging signals over one strong one.

## Mapping the committee

- Champion first. They feel the pain and engage more readily.
- Decision maker second, once the champion has engaged or after Day 14.
- Skip people who never post or engage; warm them via a colleague's thread.

## What breaks each step

- **Pick:** chasing logos without a signal; going over 30 accounts.
- **Get seen:** generic comments; commenting on everything; pitching in
  comments.
- **Connect:** a pitch or meeting ask in the note; connecting before they've
  seen your name.
- **Give first:** generic resources ("thought you'd like this article");
  anything that would work for any company.
- **Ask:** long DMs, multiple questions, opening with yourself, a 30-minute
  meeting ask as the first request.

## Safe pace

Keep LinkedIn activity human-paced: a few dozen profile views, around 20
connection requests and a handful of thoughtful comments per day at most. Never
run automation tools alongside this; the playbook depends on the touches being
genuinely personal.
